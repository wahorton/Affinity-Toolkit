2Step-by-Step Submission Process
Note (from 22 January 2026): Remission requests from registered tax agents must be submitted using the official ATO XLSX forms via Online Services for Agents (Practice Mail). Phone submission is no longer standard for agents.
  1. Ensure all tax lodgments are up to date. The ATO will generally not consider a remission while returns are outstanding.
  2. Download the official ATO GIC Remission Application Form (XLSX) using the button above. This must be submitted as an Excel (.xlsx) file — no other format is accepted.
  3. Complete the XLSX form. Use the generated text below (Section 3) to fill in the circumstances/reasons section of the form.
  4. Gather supporting evidence — e.g. medical certificates, insurance/council letters for disasters, financial statements, bank records, court orders, letters from your tax agent.
  5. Log in to the appropriate ATO portal:
    • Individuals: Online services for individuals via myGov
    • Businesses / sole traders with ABN: Online services for business
    • Tax/BAS agents: Online services for agents → Practice Mail
  6. Open a new secure mail message. Select the Topic matching your debt type (e.g. Income Tax). Select Subject: "Remission of general interest charge".
  7. Attach the completed XLSX form AND your supporting evidence. One GIC form can cover both income tax and activity statement — do not submit separately.
  8. Tick the declaration and send.
  9. Phone option (amounts ≤$2,500 or no online access): Call 13 28 61 (individuals) or 13 72 26 (businesses). Amounts over $2,500 via phone will be escalated — no immediate decision.
  10. Mail option (if online unavailable): Print and mail to Australian Taxation Office, PO Box 327, Albury NSW 2640.
  11. If declined: The ATO will write to you explaining the decision. You may submit a new request with additional information. You cannot lodge a formal objection for GIC remission decisions, but may seek judicial review in the Federal Court, or contact the Inspector-General of Taxation and Taxation Ombudsman if the decision appears unfair.

4ATO Remission Grounds Reference
✓ Likely to succeed
  • Serious illness or hospitalisation with supporting evidence
  • Natural disaster directly impacting ability to pay
  • Family and domestic violence / financial abuse
  • Death of a spouse, business partner, or sole director
  • ATO-caused delay in issuing an assessment
  • Mental health crisis
  • Theft of critical property or business records
  • Industrial action affecting operations
  • Unforeseen collapse of a major debtor
  • Reliance on incorrect professional advice with severe consequences
  • One-off event, out of pattern with strong compliance history
✗ Unlikely to succeed
  • General cash flow problems or poor trading conditions
  • A creditor paying late in normal course of business
  • General economic downturn or currency fluctuation
  • Simply forgetting or being on holiday
  • Deliberate decision to use funds for business reinvestment
  • Circumstances within the taxpayer's control
  • Repeated pattern of late payment
